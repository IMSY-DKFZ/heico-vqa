Abstract

While traditional computer vision models have historically struggled to generalize to endoscopic domains, the emergence of foundation models has shown promising cross-domain performance. In this work, we present the first large-scale study assessing the capabilities of Vision Language Models (VLMs) for endoscopic tasks with a specific focus on laparoscopic surgery. Using a diverse set of state-of-the-art models, multiple surgical datasets, and extensive human reference annotations, we address three key research questions: (1) Can current VLMs solve basic perception tasks on surgical images? (2) Can they handle advanced frame-based endoscopic scene understanding tasks? and (3) How do specialized medical VLMs compare to generalist models in this context?

Our results reveal that VLMs can effectively perform basic surgical perception tasks, such as object counting and localization, with performance levels comparable to general domain tasks. However, their performance deteriorates significantly when the tasks require medical knowledge. Notably, we find that specialized medical VLMs currently underperform compared to generalist models across both basic and advanced surgical tasks, suggesting that they are not yet optimized for the complexity of surgical environments.

These findings highlight the need for further advancements to enable VLMs to handle the unique challenges posed by surgery. Overall, our work provides important insights for the development of next-generation endoscopic AI systems and identifies key areas for improvement in medical visual language models.

Key Findings

Our comprehensive evaluation reveals important insights about VLM performance in surgical domains

Basic Tasks: Comparable Performance

VLMs can effectively perform basic surgical perception tasks like object counting and localization, with performance levels comparable to general domain tasks. Our cross-domain performance analysis demonstrates how VLMs perform across different domains, comparing surgical vs general tasks.

Medical Knowledge: Performance Drops

Performance deteriorates significantly when tasks require specific medical knowledge, highlighting limitations in domain-specific understanding. Our analysis shows how model performance systematically decreases as task complexity increases, particularly for medical-specific tasks.

Generalist vs Specialist Models

Specialized medical VLMs currently underperform compared to generalist models across both basic and advanced surgical tasks. Our direct comparison between specialized medical models and generalist VLMs reveals this surprising finding, suggesting that current medical specialization approaches may not be optimal for surgical environments.

Dataset Overview

The HeiCo-VQA-Base dataset contains surgical images and question-answer pairs specifically designed to evaluate VLM capabilities in endoscopic environments. Our dataset covers laparoscopic surgical videos from colorectal procedures, converted into a comprehensive Visual Question Answering format.

The dataset includes 10% human baseline annotations to ensure high-quality evaluation standards and reliable performance metrics.

Spatial Understanding

Understanding interactions between surgical tools and tissues or between multiple instruments.

Tasks focusing on color perception and its medical significance in surgical contexts.
